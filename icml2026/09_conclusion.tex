\section{Conclusion}

MCL watermarking turns the detection-quality tradeoff into a
closed-form policy-to-configuration map: a target false-positive
rate, a text-length floor, and a watermark budget determine the
minimum state count uniquely. The scheme inherits a scheme-independent robustness floor
$\delta^\star = 1 - 1/\sqrt{2} \approx 29.3\%$ (under the midpoint
threshold convention), and both identities extend to every
$k$-regular transition topology (Prop.~\ref{prop:kregular}). Detection is model-free and runs in
$O(n)$ hash operations, enabling third-party audit without model
access.

Empirically, a matched-budget comparison across 18 cells on
\texttt{google/gemma-3-270m-it} finds that low-entropy gating is
strictly Pareto-dominated, that detection is gate-invariant at
$\rho \geq 0.5$, and that the predicted $(1-\delta)^2$ self-healing
scaling holds on every cell to within sampling noise. The practical
implication is that the operational dial for a regulated MCL
deployment is the budget $\rho$, not a gating heuristic. The
SWEET/EWD intuition, concentrate the watermark at near-deterministic
positions, wins on neither quality nor robustness.
