\begin{abstract}
  We introduce \emph{Markov Chain Lock} (MCL), an active watermarking
  scheme for autoregressive language models that admits a closed-form
  calibration of its design parameters. MCL partitions the vocabulary
  into $S$ states via keyed SHA-256 hashing and forces a hard Markov
  transition on a fraction $\rho$ of generated positions. The
  detector is model-free, runs in $O(n)$ hash operations, and so
  enables third-party audit without LLM access.
  Theoretically, we derive an operator-facing formula mapping a
  target false-positive rate, text length, and budget to the minimum
  state count; prove a universal robustness threshold
  $\delta^{\star} = 1 - 1/\sqrt{2} \approx 29.3\%$ (independent of
  $(S, \rho, n)$ under the midpoint decision convention, and
  strategy-free against oracle-blind adversaries); and generalise
  both results to any $k$-regular transition topology.
  Empirically, on Google's Gemma~3 270M model across 18
  matched-budget cells (173 wiki + 20 factual prompts), three
  findings hold: (i) low-entropy gating is strictly Pareto-dominated;
  (ii) detection probability is gate-invariant at $\rho \geq 0.5$;
  (iii) post-attack $z$-scores match the predicted $(1-\delta)^2$
  scaling on every cell. The operational dial is the budget $\rho$,
  not the choice of gate.
\end{abstract}
