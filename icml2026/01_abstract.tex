\begin{abstract}
  Regulatory regimes such as the EU AI Act mandate machine-readable
  marking of synthetic text, but existing watermark detectors rely on
  the generating LM and on heuristic thresholds with no closed-form
  calibration. We introduce \emph{ChainMark} (ChainMark), an active
  watermark that partitions the vocabulary into $S$ states via keyed
  SHA-256 and forces a hard Markov transition on a fraction $\rho$ of
  positions; the detector replays the partition from the same key in
  $O(n)$ hash operations, with no LM access. We derive a closed-form
  $S^\star(n, \rho, \alpha)$ mapping a target FPR, text length, and
  budget to the minimum state count (\autoref{thm:detection}), prove a
  universal robustness threshold $\delta^\star = 1 - 1/\sqrt{2}
  \approx 29.3\%$ that is invariant in $(S,\rho,n)$
  (\autoref{thm:robustness}), and generalise both to any $k$-regular
  transition topology (\autoref{thm:k-regular}). Across three
  instruction-tuned LLMs and four domains, ChainMark strictly dominates
  KGW and SWEET under translation and random-substitution attacks at
  matched budget; an empirical-SD threshold recalibration restores
  the $1\%$ target FPR on natural-language text
  (\autoref{sec:experiments}).
\end{abstract}
