\begin{abstract}
  Regulatory regimes such as the EU AI Act mandate machine-readable
  marking of synthetic text, but existing watermark detectors rely on
  access to the generating language model and on entropy-based
  heuristics with no closed-form calibration to a target false-positive
  rate.
  We introduce \emph{Markov Chain Lock} (MCL), an active watermark that
  partitions the vocabulary into $S$ states via keyed SHA-256 hashing
  and forces a hard Markov transition on a fraction $\rho$ of generated
  positions; the detector replays the partition from the same secret
  key and is therefore model-free, running in $O(n)$ hash operations.
  Theoretically, we derive an operator-facing formula mapping a target
  false-positive rate, text length, and budget to the minimum state
  count $S^\star$, prove a universal robustness threshold
  $\delta^\star = 1 - 1/\sqrt{2} \approx 29.3\%$ that is independent of
  $(S, \rho, n)$ under the midpoint decision convention, and generalise
  both results to any $k$-regular transition topology
  (\autoref{thm:detection}, \autoref{thm:robustness},
  \autoref{thm:k-regular}).
  Empirically, we run head-to-head comparisons against KGW and SWEET
  across multiple instruction-tuned LLMs and four text domains under
  matched-budget translation and paraphrase attacks, sweep the state
  count $S$ to anchor the calibration formula on data, and validate the
  $k$-regular generalisation at $k=2$
  (\autoref{sec:experiments}).
  The closed-form calibration and model-free detector together let a
  third-party auditor verify a marked deployment from text alone,
  without LM access.
\end{abstract}
