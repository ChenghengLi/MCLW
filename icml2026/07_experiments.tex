\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We empirically (i)~compare MCL head-to-head against KGW and SWEET
at matched budget, and (ii)~validate the three principal predictions
of \autoref{thm:detection}, \autoref{thm:robustness}, and
\autoref{thm:k-regular}. The fleet spans three instruct-tuned 7--8B
models, four content domains, and three attack conditions in the
headline grid (\emph{clean}, random-substitution, ZH
back-translation); Exp.~3 extends to four NLLB pivots
(FR, DE, RU, ZH).
The campaign comprises four complementary sub-experiments.
Exp.~2 (\autoref{subsec:exp2-headline}) is the
head-to-head against the canonical KGW baseline and the SWEET
high-entropy gate. Exp.~3 (\autoref{subsec:exp3-translation})
measures robustness under machine-translation pivots. Exp.~4
(\autoref{subsec:exp4-kregular}) sanity-checks the $k$-regular
generalisation at $k{=}2$. Exp.~5
(\autoref{subsec:exp5-calibration}) anchors the closed-form FPR
calibration via a sweep over the state count $S$. All computation
runs on the EPFL RunAI cluster; per-prompt records (logits, gate
decisions, post-attack tokens, $z$-scores) stream to JSONL for
reproducibility.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{subsec:exp-setup}

\textbf{Models.} We use three instruction-tuned, openly licensed
checkpoints in the 7--8B parameter range:
\texttt{meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct},
\texttt{Qwen/Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct}, and
\texttt{mistralai/Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3}. The same secret key
$\kappa$ is fixed across every run.

\textbf{Domains.} Four prompt domains stress different entropy
regimes: \emph{code} (HumanEval problem stems), \emph{factual}
(short closed-form knowledge prompts), \emph{wiki} (open-ended
``Explain $X$\ldots'' prompts over a curated concept list), and
\emph{writing} (creative-completion prompts). Headline experiments (Exp.~2) use 100 prompts per domain.
Translation and calibration experiments (Exp.~3, Exp.~5) use a
25-prompt subsample per domain to keep the design balanced under
the larger Cartesian product. The $k$-regular sanity check (Exp.~4)
uses 100 prompts on the wiki domain only.

\textbf{Generation.} Temperature $T = 0.7$, top-$p = 1$, token
budget $n = 200$ at every cell. Prompt order is determined by a
deterministic seed so the same indices are watermarked, attacked,
and detected across methods.

\textbf{MCL configuration.} Unless stated otherwise, MCL runs use
$S = 5$ states, target gate budget $\rho = 0.5$, the
high-entropy gate $G_{H_{\mathrm{high}}}$, and clockwork
($k{=}1$) topology. \autoref{subsec:exp4-kregular} varies $k$ and
\autoref{subsec:exp5-calibration} varies $S$.

\textbf{Detection.} The detection statistic and the threshold
$z_\alpha$ are taken directly from \autoref{thm:detection};
the same threshold is reused across methods for fair comparison.

\subsection{Headline Comparison: MCL vs KGW vs SWEET}
\label{subsec:exp2-headline}

The headline experiment (Exp.~2) is a $3 \times 3 \times 4 \times 3$
factorial: 3 watermarking methods $\times$ 3 models $\times$ 4
domains $\times$ 3 attack conditions, with 100 prompts per
(method, model, domain) cell.

\textbf{Methods.} \emph{MCL} is configured at the canonical
operating point $S{=}5$, $\rho = 0.5$, gate $G_{H_{\mathrm{high}}}$,
clockwork ($k{=}1$). \emph{KGW}~\citep{kirchenbauer2023watermark}
uses its standard parameters $\gamma = 0.5$ and logit bias
$\delta_{\mathrm{KGW}} = 2$. \emph{SWEET}~\citep{lee2023sweet} retains the same
$\gamma = 0.5$ and $\delta_{\mathrm{KGW}} = 2$, but applies the
watermark only at positions whose token-level entropy exceeds a
threshold calibrated to gate $\rho = 0.5$ of positions. This is
the high-entropy gate that aligns with our $G_{H_{\mathrm{high}}}$,
instantiated in the green/red-list scheme.

\textbf{Attacks.} Each generated text is scored under three
conditions: \emph{clean} (no attack); \emph{random-substitution} at
effective edit distance $\delta_{\mathrm{eff}} = 0.20$; and
\emph{back-translation} via NLLB-200 EN$\to$ZH$\to$EN. For the
back-translation, the empirical $\delta_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is recorded
per output via token-edit distance to the original.

\textbf{Reporting.} The primary metric is true-positive rate at a
calibrated $\mathrm{FPR} = 0.01$ (TPR@$1\%$), computed against a
matched non-watermarked baseline drawn from the same model,
domain, and prompt set.

\subsection{Robustness Decay under Translation}
\label{subsec:exp3-translation}

Exp.~3 tests the closed-form $(1-\delta)^2$ scaling of
\autoref{thm:robustness} on real machine-translation attacks.
Each MCL-watermarked output (3 models $\times$ 4 domains $\times$
25 prompts $\times$ $n{=}200$ tokens; Mistral$\times$code did not
complete in the cluster window, see \autoref{tab:translation-pivots})
is round-tripped through NLLB-200~\citep{nllbteam2022nllb} on four
pivot languages: French, German, Russian, and Chinese. The empirical effective edit
distance $\delta_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is computed from the token-edit
distance between the watermarked output and its post-pivot
counterpart, giving each generation a continuous $\delta$ value
rather than a single nominal rate.

\autoref{thm:robustness} predicts that the post-attack $z$-score
satisfies $z_{\mathrm{post}}/z_{\mathrm{clean}} = (1 -
\delta_{\mathrm{eff}})^2$ in expectation, modulo finite-sample
Bernoulli noise. We plot the observed ratio against the
predicted $(1-\delta_{\mathrm{eff}})^2$ on a single set of axes,
overlaying the slope-1 identity.

% [Translation-decay figure consolidated into fig:exp-overview at top of section.]

The observed ratios sit at or above the
$(1-\delta_{\mathrm{eff}})^2$ diagonal across pivots, models, and
domains, anchoring the universal half-life $\delta^\star = 1 -
1/\sqrt{2} \approx 0.293$ empirically and confirming our robustness
bound is conservative rather than tight. The claim is stronger than
synthetic random-substitution attacks alone can support, since
NLLB-200 introduces semantically coherent rewrites rather than
i.i.d.\ token noise.

\subsection{$k$-Regular Generalisation}
\label{subsec:exp4-kregular}

Exp.~4 verifies the $k$-regular extension of MCL predicted by
\autoref{thm:k-regular}. Under any $k$-regular topology,
the random-baseline fingerprint mass is $p_0 = k/S$, the detection
$z$-score scales as in \autoref{thm:detection} with this updated
null mean, and the universal post-attack $\delta^\star$ is invariant
in $k$. We instantiate $k = 2$ (each state has exactly two allowed
successors) at $S = 5$, predicting $p_0 = 2/5 = 0.40$. The protocol
is the wiki domain (100 prompts) for each of the three models, with
ZH back-translation as the only attack condition --- the most
frequent attack regime on the open web.

\subsection{Empirical Calibration Anchor (S sweep)}
\label{subsec:exp5-calibration}

Exp.~5 anchors the closed-form FPR calibration of
\autoref{thm:detection} empirically. We sweep $S \in \{2, 3, 5,
7, 11\}$ across the three models, with 4 domains $\times$ 25
prompts $\times$ $n{=}200$ tokens per cell. Each cell records both
a watermarked generation (MCL at the canonical operating point,
varying only $S$) and a non-watermarked baseline drawn from the
same prompt with the same model. The non-watermarked outputs feed
the empirical FPR estimate at the analytic threshold $z_\alpha$;
the watermarked outputs feed the TPR.

\autoref{thm:detection} predicts that the null-distribution
$z$-score satisfies a Hoeffding-style upper bound
$\mathrm{FPR} \leq \exp(-2 z_\alpha^2)$ on i.i.d.\ random token
sequences (proof in \autoref{app:proofs}). The minimum admissible
state count $S^\star$ for a given target $\alpha$ is the closed
form derived in \autoref{app:proofs}; the matching Gaussian-tail
calibration of \autoref{thm:detection} is what we use throughout
the body for $z_\alpha$.

closed-form derivation lives in \autoref{app:proofs}.
