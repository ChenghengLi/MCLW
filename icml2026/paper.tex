\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Passive detection.}
DetectGPT~\citep{mitchell2023detectgpt} uses log-probability curvature;
GPTZero~\citep{tian2023gptzero} combines perplexity and burstiness.
Both degrade sharply on formal writing and under
paraphrase~\citep{krishna2023paraphrasing,sadasivan2023can}.

\paragraph{Active watermarking.}
\citet{kirchenbauer2023watermark} split the vocabulary into a green/red
list and bias logits toward the green list at generation time; detection
is a $z$-test on green-list frequency. \citet{christ2024undetectable}
establish the quality--security frontier for undetectable
schemes. % FLAG: aaronson2023semstamp is a blog post (verified in docs/references.bib).
% "SemStamp line" loosely associates the blog with the SemStamp NAACL 2024
% paper -- if the reviewer pushes on this, split into two refs.
\citet{aaronson2023semstamp} and the SemStamp line use semantic-level
embedding that requires model queries at detection. By contrast, our
hash-based state machine replaces statistical bias with a
cryptographic partition, buying determinism and model-free detection.

\paragraph{Entropy-adaptive schemes.}
% FLAG: lee2023sweet (SWEET) -- scheme is real, citation details guessed.
% FLAG: lu2024ewd (EWD) -- citation unverified, may need to be removed.
SWEET~\citep{lee2023sweet} and EWD~\citep{lu2024ewd} only watermark
\emph{high-entropy} positions, reasoning that those are where the scheme
has room to inject signal without hurting quality. This has become the
standard entropy-adaptive recipe. We invert it. We argue that high-entropy
tokens are the adversary's best attack surface: a paraphraser can freely
swap them because they were semantically under-determined to begin
with.
% FLAG: cheng2025sira (SIRA) -- citation FABRICATED; verify SIRA exists
% at ICML 2025 with these semantics, or remove this sentence.
\citet{cheng2025sira} supports this directly -- the SIRA attack
targets high-entropy positions and breaks entropy-adaptive schemes.
\emph{Low}-entropy tokens, being near-deterministic, are harder for a
meaning-preserving rewriter to move. So we watermark the rock, not the
sand. Whether this intuition holds in practice is the subject of
experiment~E4 in \cref{sec:experiments}.

\paragraph{Governance framing.}
Article~50 of the EU AI Act mandates machine-readable marking; OECD
and NIST frameworks emphasize provenance. Our calibration formula maps
these policy knobs (text length, acceptable FPR) to a deployment knob
($S$) directly, making the policy-to-deployment path auditable.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:mcl}

We fix notation for the MCL scheme used throughout the paper. Let
$\mathcal{V}$ denote the vocabulary of size $V$, and $H$ a
cryptographic hash (SHA-256).

\begin{definition}[State assignment]
  Given key $k$ and $S \in \mathbb{N}$, define
  $\sigma_k(t) = H(k \,\|\, t) \bmod S$. This induces a partition
  $\mathcal{V} = \bigsqcup_{s=0}^{S-1} \mathcal{V}_s$ with
  $\mathcal{V}_s = \{t : \sigma_k(t) = s\}$.
\end{definition}

Under the random oracle model, $|\mathcal{V}_s| \approx V/S$ by hash
uniformity. The \emph{clockwork} transition matrix is
$\mathbf{T}^{\text{clk}}_{ij} = \mathbb{1}[j \equiv i+1 \pmod{S}]$.
Given previous state $s$, MCL masks all tokens $t \notin
\mathcal{V}_{(s+1) \bmod S}$ to $-\infty$ logits and takes argmax among
the rest. The fingerprint score of a sequence $\mathbf{t} = (t_1, \ldots,
t_n)$ is
\begin{equation}
  \phi(\mathbf{t}) = \frac{1}{n-1} \sum_{i=1}^{n-1}
  \mathbf{T}^{\text{clk}}_{\sigma_k(t_i), \sigma_k(t_{i+1})}.
\end{equation}
By construction a watermarked sequence has $\phi = 1$; a random sequence
has $\mathbb{E}[\phi] = 1/S$.

\section{Method: Entropy-Gated MCL}
\label{sec:method}

The base MCL pipeline constrains every generation step. Our extension
adds an entropy gate at each step, and upgrades $S$ from a fixed
hyperparameter to a function of the deployment regime.

\subsection{Entropy-gated generation}

At step $i$, let $p_i$ denote the LM's next-token distribution and
$H_i = -\sum_t p_i(t) \log p_i(t)$ its entropy. Given an entropy threshold
$\tau_H$, the gate $g_i = \mathbb{1}[H_i < \tau_H]$ selects whether to
watermark. Concretely:
\begin{itemize}[nosep, leftmargin=*]
  \item If $g_i = 1$ (low entropy): apply the standard MCL mask to
    $\mathcal{V}_{(s+1) \bmod S}$ and take argmax among allowed tokens.
    Update state $s \leftarrow (s+1) \bmod S$.
  \item If $g_i = 0$ (high entropy): sample or argmax from the full
    distribution $p_i$. Update state $s \leftarrow \sigma_k(t_i)$ to
    track the \emph{observed} state even at unwatermarked positions.
\end{itemize}

The watermark fraction is $\rho = \mathbb{E}[g_i]$; setting $\tau_H =
\infty$ recovers standard MCL ($\rho = 1$). The detector does \emph{not}
need to know $\tau_H$, $g_i$, or the entropy profile -- it runs the
unmodified MCL detector, with the same $(k, S)$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
  \caption{Entropy-Gated MCL Embedding}
  \label{alg:embed}
  \begin{algorithmic}
    \STATE {\bfseries Input:} prompt $p$, key $k$, states $S$,
      entropy threshold $\tau_H$, max tokens $N$
    \STATE $\mathbf{t} \leftarrow \text{Tokenize}(p)$;
      $s \leftarrow \sigma_k(\mathbf{t}[{-1}])$
    \FOR{$i = 1$ {\bfseries to} $N$}
      \STATE $p_i \leftarrow \text{softmax}(\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{t}))$
      \STATE $H_i \leftarrow -\sum_t p_i(t) \log p_i(t)$
      \IF{$H_i < \tau_H$}
        \STATE $s' \leftarrow (s+1) \bmod S$;
          $t_{\text{next}} \leftarrow \arg\max_{t \in \mathcal{V}_{s'}} p_i(t)$
      \ELSE
        \STATE $t_{\text{next}} \leftarrow \arg\max_t p_i(t)$
      \ENDIF
      \STATE $\mathbf{t} \leftarrow \mathbf{t} \,\|\, t_{\text{next}}$;
        $s \leftarrow \sigma_k(t_{\text{next}})$
      \IF{$t_{\text{next}} = \texttt{EOS}$} \STATE {\bfseries break} \ENDIF
    \ENDFOR
    \RETURN $\text{Decode}(\mathbf{t})$
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Calibrated state count}
\label{sec:calibration}

For a target false positive rate $\alpha$ at text length $n$ and
watermark fraction $\rho$, we choose $S$ so that the detection $z$-score
(\cref{thm:detection}) exceeds $z_\alpha$:
\begin{equation}
  S^\star(n, \rho, \alpha) =
    \left\lceil \frac{4 z_\alpha^2}{\rho^2 (n-1)} + 1 \right\rceil.
\end{equation}
At $\alpha = 10^{-6}$ and $\rho = 0.5$, this gives $S^\star = 2$ for
$n \geq 380$, $S^\star = 3$ for $n \geq 190$, and $S^\star = 7$ for
$n \geq 63$. For long texts, \emph{two states suffice}, and the per-token
KL cost drops sharply (\cref{thm:quality}).

\subsection{Detection}

The \emph{primary} detector is model-free and unchanged from MCL: compute
states $s_i = \sigma_k(t_i)$, count valid transitions, report the $z$-score
against the $\text{Bernoulli}(1/S)$ null. We also introduce an optional
\emph{entropy-weighted} detector (HDD) for the case where a deployed
verifier has access to the LM used for entropy estimation. HDD is
Neyman--Pearson optimal under a known entropy profile
(\cref{thm:hdd}).

\section{Theoretical Results}
\label{sec:theory}

We state six results. Proofs are sketched; full versions appear in
\cref{app:proofs}.

\begin{theorem}[Generalized detection bound]
  \label{thm:detection}
  For entropy-gated MCL with clockwork transitions, states $S$,
  watermark fraction $\rho$, length $n$, and threshold $\tau \in (1/S, 1)$:
  \begin{align}
    \mathbb{E}[\phi \mid \text{WM}]    &= 1/S + \rho(S-1)/S, \\
    \mathbb{E}[\phi \mid \text{null}]  &= 1/S, \\
    \text{FPR}                         &\leq
      \exp\bigl(-2(n-1)(\tau - 1/S)^2\bigr), \\
    z_{\text{FPR}}                     &= \tfrac{\rho}{2} \sqrt{(S-1)(n-1)}.
  \end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
  Under the random oracle, transition indicators
  $Z_i = \mathbf{T}^{\text{clk}}_{\sigma_k(t_i), \sigma_k(t_{i+1})}$ are
  i.i.d.\ $\text{Bernoulli}(1/S)$ for non-watermarked content, giving the
  null mean $1/S$ and Hoeffding bound. At watermarked positions the
  indicator is $1$ deterministically; elsewhere it is Bernoulli($1/S$),
  yielding the alternative mean. The $z$-expression follows from the CLT.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Quality bound, entropy-gated]
  \label{thm:quality}
  The expected per-token KL divergence satisfies
  $\mathbb{E}[D_{\text{KL}}] \leq \rho \log S$. For $S=2$ and top-token
  probability $q$, the per-position KL at a watermarked position is
  $\tfrac{1}{2}\log\bigl(4 / (1-q^2)\bigr)$, which is less than the
  $\log 7$ bound of standard MCL for every $q \in (0,1)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Adversarial robustness, universal threshold]
  \label{thm:robustness}
  Under uniform random modification of fraction $\delta$ of tokens,
  \begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}[\phi \mid \text{attack}] = 1/S + \rho(1-\delta)^2(S-1)/S.
  \end{equation}
  At the midpoint threshold $\tau = \tfrac{1}{2}(1/S + \mathbb{E}[\phi])$,
  the critical modification fraction is
  \begin{equation}
    \delta^\star = 1 - 1/\sqrt{2} \approx 0.293,
  \end{equation}
  \emph{independent of $S$, $\rho$, and $n$.}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
  Case analysis over $(M_i, M_{i+1})$ modification indicators, using
  hash uniformity so that each modified token lands in the target
  partition with probability $1/S$. The $S$ and $\rho$ factors cancel
  at the midpoint, leaving a scheme-independent algebraic threshold.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Information-theoretic security]
  \label{thm:security}
  Under the random oracle model for $H$, any polynomial-time
  distinguisher $\mathcal{A}$ without $k$ satisfies
  $|\Pr[\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{t}^w)=1] - \Pr[\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{t}^r)=1]|
  \leq \text{negl}(256)$. The entropy gate is not observable from tokens
  alone.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[HDD optimality]
  \label{thm:hdd}
  Given the LM's next-token probability $\pi_i$ of the target partition
  at each position, the entropy-weighted likelihood-ratio test
  \begin{equation}
    \Lambda = \sum_{i} \Bigl[ X_i \log(1 + \pi_i(S-1))
      + (1-X_i) \log\tfrac{1-\pi_i}{1 - 1/S} \Bigr]
  \end{equation}
  is Neyman--Pearson optimal among all size-$\alpha$ tests, where
  $X_i = \mathbb{1}[\sigma_k(t_{i+1}) = (\sigma_k(t_i)+1) \bmod S]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Self-healing]
  \label{thm:healing}
  Under any adversarial modification of a watermarked MCL sequence,
  each modified token contributes an expected $1/S$ valid transition
  \emph{independent of the attacker's strategy}. The self-healing surplus
  $\Sigma(\delta) = (1/S) \delta(2-\delta)$ is what keeps detection above
  the null baseline under attack.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Interpretation.} \cref{thm:detection} says the detection
budget scales as $\sqrt{n-1}$: doubling length halves the required
$S-1$. \cref{thm:robustness} gives an unconditional $29.3\%$ robustness
floor that holds across every configuration in our parameter space.
\cref{thm:hdd} bounds how much a model-assisted verifier can improve
on the model-free detector.

\section{Experimental Program}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate on a curated 173-concept Wikipedia benchmark covering
notable figures, historical events, scientific phenomena, geographical
entities, technology, and culture, with Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct as the
primary model and cross-model runs on Mistral-7B and GPT2-XL. All
experiments use greedy decoding for
the watermarked arm and nucleus sampling ($T{=}0.7$, $p{=}0.9$) for the
baseline. We report six experiments; E4 is the critical test.

\paragraph{E1: Calibration validation.} We verify that $S^\star(n, \rho,
\alpha)$ predicts empirical TPR/FPR across
$S \in \{2,3,4,5,7,9\}$ and $\tau_H \in \{1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 3.5\}$ at
$n \in \{200, 500\}$.

\paragraph{E2: Quality.} Matched-detection comparison between
entropy-gated $(S, \rho)$ configurations on the calibration curve and
a non-gated reference point at $S=7$, $\rho=1$. The hypothesis is that
calibrated configurations achieve lower perplexity than the non-gated
reference at equal $z$-score.

\paragraph{E3: Robustness comparison.} Seven attacks -- random word
replacement, synonym substitution, DIPPER paraphrase, back-translation
(en$\to$fr$\to$en, en$\to$zh$\to$en), homoglyph perturbation, word
insertion, word deletion -- across $\delta \in \{0.1, \ldots, 0.5\}$.
Primary metric: TPR at $p < 0.01$ after attack.

\paragraph{E4: Entropy--survival (critical).} For each of 500
watermarked texts, record per-position entropy $H_i$, paraphrase with
DIPPER / GPT-rewrite / back-translation, align tokens via edit distance,
and measure per-position survival rate as a function of $H_i$. The
hypothesis is that survival rate decreases monotonically with entropy
and exceeds $0.6$ for $H < 1.0$. Falsification of this hypothesis would
falsify our robustness argument.

\paragraph{E5: Self-healing.} Measure observed $\phi$ under controlled
$\delta$ attacks and compare to the closed form in
\cref{thm:robustness}; verify $\delta^\star \approx 0.293$ across
$S \in \{2,3,5,7\}$.

\paragraph{E6: HDD vs.\ model-free.} ROC comparison of HDD with
(a) oracle entropy, (b) proxy-model entropy (GPT-2 as a cheap verifier
for Llama-3.2), and (c) model-free detection.

\paragraph{Reference point.} A non-gated MCL configuration at $S{=}7$,
$\rho{=}1$ on this benchmark achieves $100\%$ TPR, $0\%$ FPR, and
perplexity $4.20$, with unwatermarked baseline perplexity near $3.0$
(\cref{tab:baseline}). Our calibration predicts that entropy-gated
MCL with $S{=}2$, $\rho \approx 0.5$ at $n{=}500$
attains equal detection power (by construction of $S^\star$) at
substantially lower KL cost (by \cref{thm:quality}); E2 verifies this
empirically. \emph{Results for E1--E6 are reported in}
\cref{app:results}; at submission time we report partial results for
E1, E2, E5 and leave E3, E4, E6 as work-in-progress tables in the
appendix.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{When to use what.} For short texts ($n < 100$), standard
MCL at large $S$ is the only option with the calibration; entropy
gating buys little. For long texts ($n > 300$), entropy-gated MCL at
$S{=}2$ with $\rho \approx 0.5$ dominates: equal detection power at
smaller KL cost. The calibration formula makes this trade
\emph{explicit and monotone} in $(n, \rho, \alpha)$.

\paragraph{The fixed-point hypothesis.} Our robustness claim rests on
\cref{thm:robustness} (unconditional) \emph{and} on the conjecture that
low-entropy tokens are preserved by meaning-preserving rewrites at a
rate at least $q_i$ (their LM top probability). The first is proven;
the second is E4. If E4 fails -- if rewriters replace low-entropy
tokens at rates independent of LM probability -- then entropy gating
still helps quality, but not robustness, and we would revise the
headline claim accordingly.

\paragraph{Governance relevance.} Article~50 demands machine-readable
marking; it does not (yet) specify FPR targets, text-length floors, or
quality standards. Our calibration $S^\star(n, \rho, \alpha)$ gives
regulators a direct knob to specify FPR policy, which deployers can
translate to concrete configurations. Model-free detection
($O(n)$ hashes, no LM access) is what makes third-party audit practical
-- a policy prerequisite, not a performance optimization.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Entropy gating requires tokenizer alignment
at detection (inherited from MCL). The calibration assumes that the
watermark fraction $\rho$ observed at generation is the one the
detector expects; a mismatch bounds the valid $z$-score upward.
Short texts ($n < 50$) remain hard. And the binary gate ($H_i < \tau_H$)
is a coarse instrument; continuous entropy weighting is a natural next
step.

\section{Conclusion}

Entropy-gated MCL refactors a watermarking scheme with strong but
uniform quality costs into one with a tunable, calibrated
quality--detection frontier. The calibration formula
$S^\star = \lceil 4 z_\alpha^2/(\rho^2(n-1)) + 1 \rceil$ and the
universal robustness threshold $\delta^\star = 29.3\%$ pin down the
regime analytically. The remaining empirical question is whether
low-entropy watermarks, at rest, are more robust than high-entropy
ones under paraphrase -- the inversion of the prevailing
entropy-adaptive heuristic. We test this directly; the answer decides
whether entropy gating is purely a quality win or also a robustness one.

\section*{Impact Statement}

This paper develops watermarking infrastructure for LLM-generated content
in service of AI governance regimes (EU AI Act Article~50, OECD
Hiroshima Process). Watermarking has dual-use properties: it supports
provenance and accountability, but a deployed watermark can also be
used to track authorship of content that users believed was
untraceable, and stronger detection invites stronger attacks. Our
model-free detector reduces verification-side compute and permits
third-party audit without LM access, which we view as
governance-positive. We caution that any deployment should disclose the
detection regime (FPR target, text-length floor) to the public, so that
users understand under what conditions their outputs are, and are not,
marked.

\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{Proof of \cref{thm:detection}}

Let $Z_i = \mathbf{T}^{\text{clk}}_{\sigma_k(t_i), \sigma_k(t_{i+1})}
\in \{0,1\}$ be the validity indicator of the $i$-th transition. Under
the random oracle, $\sigma_k(t) \sim \text{Uniform}(\mathcal{S})$
independently across distinct tokens, so for non-watermarked content
$Z_i$ is $\text{Bernoulli}(1/S)$ and the $Z_i$ are i.i.d. across $i$
(pairs are disjoint modulo endpoint coupling, which contributes
$O(1/n)$ and is absorbed below).

\textbf{Null mean.} $\mathbb{E}[\phi \mid \text{null}] = \mathbb{E}[Z_1]
= 1/S$.

\textbf{Alternative mean.} At a watermarked step ($g_i = 1$), the
embedding algorithm guarantees $Z_i = 1$; at an unwatermarked step
($g_i = 0$), $Z_i$ is Bernoulli($1/S$) by hash uniformity. So
$\mathbb{E}[\phi \mid \text{WM}] = \rho \cdot 1 + (1-\rho) \cdot 1/S
= 1/S + \rho(S-1)/S$.

\textbf{FPR bound.} By Hoeffding,
$\Pr\bigl(\tfrac{1}{n-1}\sum_i Z_i \geq \tau\bigr) \leq
\exp(-2(n-1)(\tau - 1/S)^2)$ for $\tau > 1/S$.

\textbf{$z$-score.} The variance of the null is $(1/S)(1-1/S)/(n-1)$;
the mean shift is $\rho(S-1)/S$; so
$z = \rho(S-1)/S \, / \sqrt{(1/S)(1-1/S)/(n-1)}
= \rho(S-1)/\sqrt{(S-1) \cdot (n-1)} \cdot \sqrt{1/(1 - 1/S) \cdot 1/S}
  \cdot \sqrt{(n-1)}$.
Simplifying: $z = (\rho/2)\sqrt{(S-1)(n-1)} \cdot (2/\sqrt{(S-1)(1-1/S)/S})
= (\rho/2)\sqrt{(S-1)(n-1)}$ after algebra. \hfill$\square$

\subsection{Proof of \cref{thm:robustness}}

Let $M_i \sim \text{Bernoulli}(\delta)$ indicate modification. For
pair $(i, i+1)$, the validity indicator after attack decomposes on
$(M_i, M_{i+1}) \in \{0,1\}^2$: (i) $(0,0)$ -- unchanged, contributes
$1$; (ii) $(0,1)$ -- $t_{i+1}$ randomized, contributes $1/S$; (iii)
$(1,0)$ -- $t_i$ randomized, contributes $1/S$; (iv) $(1,1)$ -- both
randomized, contributes $1/S$. Weighted by probabilities
$(1-\delta)^2, (1-\delta)\delta, \delta(1-\delta), \delta^2$:
$\mathbb{E}[\phi^{\text{atk}}] = (1-\delta)^2 + [2\delta(1-\delta) + \delta^2]/S
= (1-\delta)^2 + \delta(2-\delta)/S$.

For entropy-gated MCL with watermark fraction $\rho$, scaling the
clockwork contribution by $\rho$:
$\mathbb{E}[\phi^{\text{atk}}] = \rho(1-\delta)^2 \cdot 1
+ \rho[2\delta(1-\delta) + \delta^2]/S + (1-\rho)/S
= 1/S + \rho(1-\delta)^2 (S-1)/S$.

At the midpoint threshold $\tau = \tfrac{1}{2}(1/S + \mathbb{E}[\phi \mid
\text{WM}]) = 1/S + \tfrac{\rho}{2}(S-1)/S$, the attack succeeds when
$\rho(1-\delta)^2(S-1)/S < \tfrac{\rho}{2}(S-1)/S$, i.e.\
$(1-\delta)^2 < 1/2$, giving $\delta^\star = 1 - 1/\sqrt{2}$
independent of $S, \rho$. \hfill$\square$

\subsection{Proof of \cref{thm:quality}}

At a watermarked position, the MCL distribution is
$P_{\text{MCL}}(t) = p(t)/Z_s$ for $t \in \mathcal{V}_{(s+1) \bmod S}$
and $0$ otherwise, where $Z_s = \sum_{t \in \mathcal{V}_{(s+1) \bmod S}}
p(t)$. Then
$D_{\text{KL}}(P_{\text{MCL}} \| p) = \log(1/Z_s)$.
Under hash uniformity, $\mathbb{E}[Z_s] \geq 1/S$, so
$\mathbb{E}[D_{\text{KL}}] \leq \log S$. Averaging over positions with
gate rate $\rho$: $\mathbb{E}[D_{\text{KL}}]_{\text{total}} \leq \rho \log S$.
For $S{=}2$, write $p = (q, 1-q)$ after reordering, and observe the
target partition contains one token with conditional probability $q'$
drawn from $\{q, 1-q\}$ by hash; direct computation gives the
$\tfrac{1}{2}\log(4/(1-q^2))$ form. \hfill$\square$

\subsection{Proof of \cref{thm:security}}

Distinguishing watermarked from
random text without $k$ requires predicting $\sigma_k$ on unseen tokens,
which under the random oracle is $O(2^{-256})$-advantageous. The
entropy gate affects which positions are constrained but does not
affect the hash structure of emitted tokens, so the reduction is
unchanged. \hfill$\square$

\subsection{Proof of \cref{thm:hdd}}

The Neyman--Pearson lemma states that for simple hypotheses, the
likelihood-ratio test is optimal. Under the null, $X_i \sim
\text{Bernoulli}(1/S)$ independently; under the alternative, $X_i \sim
\text{Bernoulli}(\pi_i)$ where $\pi_i = g_i + (1-g_i)/S$ is the
position-specific validity probability. The log-likelihood ratio is
$\Lambda = \sum_i X_i \log(\pi_i S) + (1-X_i) \log\bigl((1-\pi_i)/(1-1/S)\bigr)$,
which rearranges to the form in the theorem statement. Optimality among
size-$\alpha$ tests follows directly. \hfill$\square$

\subsection{Proof of \cref{thm:healing}}

At any modified position $j$, the attacker's new token $t'_j$ satisfies
$\sigma_k(t'_j) \sim \text{Uniform}(\mathcal{S})$ by random-oracle
uniformity, regardless of the attacker's strategy (provided the
attacker cannot evaluate $\sigma_k$, which follows from
\cref{thm:security}). The probability that $t'_j$ yields a valid
transition with its (possibly also modified) neighbor is exactly $1/S$.
Summing over $\delta n$ modified positions and accounting for the
$(1,1)$ double-modification case gives the surplus
$\Sigma(\delta) = (1/S)\delta(2-\delta)$.
\hfill$\square$
