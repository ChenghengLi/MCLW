\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Regulatory regimes are now committing to machine-readable marking of
AI-generated content. EU AI Act Article~50~\citep{euaiact2024}, with
enforcement beginning August 2026, mandates that providers of
generative AI systems embed detectable signals in their outputs;
parallel requirements appear in the OECD Hiroshima
Process~\citep{oecdhiroshima2024} and in the EU's draft Code of
Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated
Content~\citep{eucodepractice2025}. The question these regimes leave
unspecified is \emph{what counts as compliance}: at what false
positive rate, for what text length, at what quality cost.

\paragraph{The tradeoff that no one is naming.}
Active watermarking schemes sit on a multidimensional frontier among
detection power, robustness to modification, text-quality cost, and
generation-time complexity. Current deployments choose a point on this
frontier privately. A regulator who writes ``outputs shall be marked''
has no mechanism to specify \emph{where} on the frontier to sit, and
an auditor has no basis on which to evaluate a deployer's choice. The
consequence is that Article~50 is straightforward to comply with
\emph{nominally} (mark something, anything) and essentially
unauditable \emph{substantively}.

Existing watermarking approaches do not fix this. Passive
detection~\citep{mitchell2023detectgpt,tian2023gptzero} reads
statistical traces in the text and suffers high false positive rates
on technical prose, with no controllable frontier at all.
Green--red-list watermarking~\citep{kirchenbauer2023watermark} biases
logits during generation but requires temperature sampling and
degrades under paraphrase~\citep{krishna2023paraphrasing}. Schemes
establishing the undetectability
frontier~\citep{christ2024undetectable} are informative bounds but
not directly deployable. None expose a calibration map from
regulatory parameters to deployed configuration.

\paragraph{Markov Chain Lock watermarking.}
We present \emph{Markov Chain Lock} (MCL) watermarking: a scheme
that partitions the vocabulary into $S$ states via
$\sigma_k(t) = H(k \,\|\, t) \bmod S$ (SHA-256) and constrains
generation to follow a clockwork transition $s \to (s+1) \bmod S$.
Watermarking is \emph{entropy-gated}: the constraint applies only at
positions where the model is confident, leaving high-entropy positions
unconstrained so that the generator's expressive budget is spent on
positions where fluency is decided. The scheme has three properties
that suit it to operationalising Article~50:
\begin{itemize}[nosep, leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Model-free detection.} Verification maps each token to
    a state via the shared key and counts valid transitions in $O(n)$
    hash operations. A third-party auditor needs neither the original
    model nor a proxy LM.
  \item \textbf{Exponential FPR.} A watermarked sequence has score
    $1$ by construction; random sequences have score $1/S$ with
    Hoeffding bound $\exp(-2(n-1)(\tau - 1/S)^2)$ on the FPR.
  \item \textbf{Greedy decoding compatibility.} The constraint is a
    logit mask, not a temperature bias, so generation remains
    deterministic.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Calibration: the regulatory knob.}
The core analytical result is a closed-form mapping from deployment
regime to configuration. Under the null, the detection $z$-score is
$z = (\rho/2)\sqrt{(S-1)(n-1)}$ where $\rho$ is the watermark
fraction. Inverting for the minimum $S$ at which a target FPR $\alpha$
is met gives
\begin{equation}
  S^\star(n, \rho, \alpha)
    = \left\lceil \frac{4 z_\alpha^2}{\rho^2 (n-1)} + 1 \right\rceil.
  \label{eq:calibration-intro}
\end{equation}
A regulator choosing a target FPR $\alpha$ and a text-length floor
$n_{\min}$ reads off a unique minimum configuration; a deployer can
only adjust $\rho$ upward, trading quality for margin, but cannot
undercut detection. This is, we argue, the regulatory knob Article~50
has been missing.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep, leftmargin=*]
  \item An end-to-end active watermarking scheme (MCL) with
    SHA-256 state partitioning, clockwork transitions, entropy-gated
    embedding (\S\ref{sec:method}), and model-free $O(n)$ detection.
  \item A calibration formula (\cref{eq:calibration-intro}) derived
    from a generalized detection bound $z = (\rho/2)\sqrt{(S-1)(n-1)}$
    (Thm.~\ref{thm:detection}), mapping regulatory parameters to
    deployed configurations.
  \item A universal robustness threshold
    $\delta^\star = 1 - 1/\sqrt{2} \approx 29.3\%$
    (Thm.~\ref{thm:robustness}) at which detection crosses the
    midpoint, independent of $S$, $\rho$, and $n$ -- a
    scheme-independent floor regulators can reference directly.
  \item A Neyman--Pearson optimal entropy-weighted detector
    (Thm.~\ref{thm:hdd}) that strictly dominates the model-free
    detector when the entropy profile is observable, and a
    self-healing theorem (Thm.~\ref{thm:healing}) showing each
    adversarial modification contributes $1/S$ in expectation to the
    valid-transition count.
  \item An experimental program (\S\ref{sec:experiments}) with one
    critical test: whether low-entropy tokens survive paraphrasing at
    higher rates than high-entropy ones.
    % FLAG: lee2023sweet / lu2024ewd citations are unverified.
    If yes, entropy gating is also a robustness win and the
    SWEET~\citep{lee2023sweet}~/~EWD~\citep{lu2024ewd} heuristic
    (watermark the high-entropy tokens) is inverted; if no, we revise
    the robustness claim accordingly.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Governance framing.}
The contribution is as much about \emph{operationalising} Article~50
as it is about watermarking. The calibration formula turns an opaque
deployer choice into a two-number policy specification
$(\alpha, n_{\min})$ that an auditor can check. The model-free
$O(n)$ detector makes that audit feasible without model access. And
the universal $\delta^\star$ gives regulators a scheme-independent
robustness benchmark. \S\ref{sec:discussion} lays out a minimum viable
Article~50 specification in concrete numbers.
